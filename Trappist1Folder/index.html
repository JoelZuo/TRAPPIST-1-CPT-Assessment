TRAPPIST - 1 System

Explore the stellar system known as TRAPPIST - 1, complete with 7 exoplanets orbiting a signal red dwarf star 40 light years away from earth.

Explore TRAPPISTS B - H each with it's own unique composition and quirks for you to read

SPECULOOS - 1

The center of the system is an ultra-cool red dwarf star, only slightly larger than Jupiter and roughly 9% of our Sun's mass. It is cool, faint, and emits radiation mostly in the infrared

TRAPPIST - 1b

The innermost planet orbits extremely close to the star, completing a full orbit in just 1.5 days. Extremely hot and likely tidally locked, observations suggest it is a bare, atmosphere-free rocky world.

TRAPPIST - 1c

Receiving similar radiation to Venus, TRAPPIST-1c is a hot world orbiting its star in 2.4 days. Recent observations indicate it likely lacks a thick, heavy atmosphere, leaving it warm and dry.

TRAPPIST - 1d

Located near the inner edge of the habitable zone, TRAPPIST-1d is the lightest planet in the system. It receives about the same energy from its star as Earth does from the Sun.

TRAPPIST - 1e

Often considered the most Earth-like of the group, TRAPPIST-1e sits firmly in the middle of the habitable zone. It has a similar size, density, and surface gravity to Earth, making it a prime target for studying surface water potential.

TRAPPIST - 1f

Orbiting in the outer region of the habitable zone, TRAPPIST-1f takes about 9 Earth days to complete an orbit. It is cooler than Earth and could potentially support liquid water under the right atmospheric conditions.

TRAPPIST - 1g

As the largest planet in the system, TRAPPIST-1g lies near the outer edge of the habitable zone. Cold and tidally locked, it receives less light than Mars does in our solar system.

TRAPPIST - 1h

The outermost world orbits at the frozen fringes of the system, taking nearly 19 days per orbit. Far from its star's heat, TRAPPIST-1h is likely an icy world covered in global glaciers.
